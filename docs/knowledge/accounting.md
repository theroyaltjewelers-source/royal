## Not a CPA

## Double-entry accounting

Every transaction affects at least two accounts, and debits equal credits. Assets equal liabilities plus equity. A journal entry records the date, the accounts, the debits and credits, and a description; entries post to the general ledger, which holds every account's history.

## Chart of accounts

The chart of accounts lists every account by type: assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, cost of goods sold, and expenses. A clear chart (for example separate accounts for metal, stones, outside setting and casting) makes job costing and margins readable.

## Financial statements

The income statement shows revenue, costs and profit over a period. The balance sheet shows assets, liabilities and equity at a moment. The cash-flow statement explains the change in cash through operating, investing and financing activities. Net income links the income statement to equity; the cash-flow statement reconciles net income to the change in cash.

## Accrual and cash basis

Cash basis records revenue when cash is received and expenses when paid. Accrual basis records revenue when earned and expenses when incurred, matching costs to the revenue they produce. Deposits received before work is delivered are a liability (customer deposits, deferred revenue) until earned under accrual.

## Revenue recognition concepts

Revenue is recognized when the promised good or service is transferred to the client. For a custom piece that is usually at delivery, not when a deposit arrives. The applicable rules depend on the reporting basis; the House's accountant decides the policy.

## Inventory and COGS

Inventory is goods held for sale or for use in production: metal, stones, findings, finished pieces. Cost of goods sold is the cost of what was sold: beginning inventory plus purchases minus ending inventory. Inventory sits on the balance sheet until sold, then moves to COGS. Valuation methods (specific identification, FIFO, average cost) change reported margin when prices move; specific identification suits unique stones.

## Job costing

Job costing accumulates the costs of each commission: materials, stones, direct labor and outside services, plus an overhead allocation. It shows the true margin of each job and which kinds of work are profitable.

## Accounts receivable and payable

A/R is what clients owe; A/P is what the House owes vendors. Both should be aged and reviewed weekly. Payment terms on each side shape cash.

## Depreciation and amortization

Long-lived equipment (casting machines, lasers, display cases) is capitalized and depreciated over its useful life, spreading its cost. Amortization does the same for intangible assets. Tax depreciation rules differ from book rules and change; they are a question for current sources.

## Expense classification

Classify spending consistently: direct job costs versus overhead, and capital purchases versus expenses. Consistent classification makes margins comparable month to month.

## Bank reconciliation

A bank reconciliation matches the ledger's cash balance to the bank statement, explaining differences (deposits in transit, outstanding checks, fees, errors). Done monthly, it catches errors and fraud early.

## Month-end close

Close the month by recording all revenue and costs, reconciling bank, card and loan accounts, reviewing A/R and A/P, counting or confirming inventory, posting accruals, and reviewing the statements for anything unexpected.

## Internal controls and audit trail

Separate duties for approving, paying and recording. Keep documentation for every transaction (invoice, receipt, approval). Never edit records without a trail; reverse and re-enter instead. ROYAL's own audit log follows the same principle.

## Sales tax, payroll and income tax concepts

Sales tax is collected from clients on taxable sales and remitted to the state; what is taxable, the rates and the filing schedule are set by each state and change. Payroll involves withholding, employer taxes and filings on fixed schedules. Business income tax depends on the entity type. Estimated tax payments may be required during the year. Every one of these is time-sensitive: I research the current rule from official sources rather than relying on this reference.
