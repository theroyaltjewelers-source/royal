## Process design

Map the process as it actually runs, step by step, with owners, inputs, outputs and handoffs. Most delay hides in waiting between steps, not in the work itself.

## Bottlenecks and capacity

Throughput is limited by the slowest step (the constraint). Improving any other step does not increase output. Find the constraint (often one setter, one caster, or the founder's approvals), keep it fed and protected, and add capacity there first.

## Cycle time and lead time

Lead time is from order to delivery as the client experiences it; cycle time is the working time of a step. Little's Law: average work in progress equals throughput times average time in the system. Too much work in progress lengthens every job.

## Queue management

Limit work in progress, sequence by due date and value, and make queues visible. Expedite only with a rule, or everything becomes urgent.

## Quality control

Inspect against the approved design and a written standard at defined points (after casting, after setting, final QC). Track rework: its rate and cause reveal process problems. Prevention beats inspection.

## Vendor management

Track each vendor's on-time rate, quality, cost and communication. Keep a backup for critical work. Agree on lead times in writing and confirm dates rather than assuming them.

## Procurement and inventory

Buy to need with safety stock for critical, long-lead items. Commodity materials (gold, platinum) carry price risk; buying at order or hedging the quote protects margin.

## SOPs and handoffs

A standard operating procedure states the trigger, the steps, the owner, the quality check and the record. Clean handoffs name who has the work next and when.

## Operational KPIs

Track on-time delivery rate, stage aging (how long jobs sit in each stage), rework rate, vendor on-time rate, capacity utilization at the constraint, and commitments kept. Review weekly; act on exceptions.

## Project management

Each project has a scope, a timeline with milestones, an owner, dependencies and a risk list. Critical path is the longest chain of dependent tasks; a delay on it delays delivery. Communicate early when a date moves.

## Continuous improvement and exceptions

Fix the cause, not the instance: when something goes wrong, ask why until the process cause appears, change the process, and check that it worked.
